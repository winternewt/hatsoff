# Novelty Scan: HAT Aperiodic Monotile + Site-Dilution + Gallai–Edmonds Zero Modes

## TL;DR

Gade–Wegner concerns the Anderson localization length / multifractal scaling of generic random-hopping eigenstates at E=0; your p_c concerns the geometric/combinatorial spanning of the topologically-protected zero-mode support (the R-type regions of the Gallai–Edmonds decomposition). Two adjacent papers — Roche Carrasco, Schirmann, Mordret, Grushin, PRL 135, 236603 (Dec 2025) on uniform Anderson disorder of a *Chern* tight-binding Hat-family model, and Daníelsson & Sigurðsson, arXiv:2605.29023 (May 2026) on multifractality of a *continuum polariton* Hat — must be cited but do not preempt you.
- **Both arXiv IDs verify as real and correctly attributed.** Bhola–Damle 2512.23639 (Dec 2025) treats the site-diluted *kagome* lattice (periodic, claw-free), with no aperiodic content. ## Key Findings

### Q1 — Is p_c → 0 generic chiral-class physics? VERDICT: NO.

The instinct that "chiral 2D zero modes are localized at any finite disorder" conflates two genuinely different objects.

1. **Gade–Wegner / chiral universality (classes AIII, BDI, CII).** König, Ostrovsky, Protopopov & Mirlin (arXiv:1201.6288, Phys. Rev. B 85, 195130 (2012), "Metal-insulator transition in 2D random fermion systems of chiral symmetry classes") confirm the original Gade–Wegner picture: 2D chiral random-hopping models have a divergent DOS at E=0, ρ(ε) ∼ |ε|^{−1} exp(−c|ln ε|^{1/2}), and weak-localization corrections vanish to all orders in perturbation theory; localization emerges only through non-perturbative vortex excitations of the σ-model, very slowly. The associated zero mode for an odd number of sites (Brouwer, Furusaki, Hatsugai, Morita, Mudry & Racine, PRB 66, 014204 (2002) / cond-mat/0201580, "Zero-modes in the random hopping model") has a localization length that depends sensitively on boundary conditions and "can take values anywhere between the mean free path and infinity." The multifractal-zero-mode study of Kleftogiannis & Evangelou (arXiv:1304.5968, 2013, "Multifractal zero mode for disordered graphene") shows D_2 evolves continuously with disorder strength. In short, the chiral 2D E=0 state is anomalous — critical / multifractal, not generically localized at any finite disorder.

2. **Matching / Dulmage–Mendelsohn support percolation.** Bhola, Biswas, Islam & Damle, PRX 12, 021058 (2022) / arXiv:2007.04974 prove that on site-diluted square and honeycomb lattices the *geometric* support of the topologically-protected zero modes (the R-regions of the Dulmage–Mendelsohn decomposition) percolates at a *finite* vacancy density. This is a combinatorial statement about which sites must host monomers of any maximum matching; it is Hamiltonian-disorder-independent.

**Reconciliation.** Both statements are simultaneously true because they answer different questions: Gade–Wegner concerns the quantum localization length / multifractal scaling of generic states at E=0 under random hopping on a full lattice, whereas the matching p_c concerns the spatial connectivity of forced-monomer sites under site dilution — a topological/combinatorial property of the diluted graph. On bipartite diluted lattices p_c is finite (Bhola–Damle 2022). On periodic non-bipartite diluted lattices (Bhola–Damle, arXiv:2311.05634, "Chaotic percolation in the random geometry of maximum-density dimer packings") R-region percolation also occurs at finite vacancy density, with multiple thresholds in d=3 and chaotic violations of self-averaging in the percolated phase. Your finding p_c → 0 for the hat is therefore a genuine departure from the entire periodic catalog, not a restatement of any known chiral-class folklore.

### Q2 — Is "aperiodic monotile + matching/Gallai–Edmonds zero modes under dilution" occupied? VERDICT: NO.

A focused subagent search over 2023–2026 preprints, journals, theses, and APS abstracts produced **no paper combining aperiodic tilings with matching-theory (Dulmage–Mendelsohn or Gallai–Edmonds) zero-mode analysis under dilution.** The closest-adjacent works are:

- **Flicker, Simon, Parameswaran, PRX 10, 011005 (2020), "Classical Dimers on Penrose Tilings"** — maximum matchings on the clean rhombic Penrose tiling; introduces the impermeable monomer membranes; bipartite, undiluted.
- **Lloyd, Biswas, Simon, Parameswaran, Flicker, PRB 106, 094202 (2022), "Statistical mechanics of dimers on quasiperiodic Ammann–Beenker tilings"** — clean Ammann–Beenker admits perfect matchings.
- **Singh, Flicker, PRB 109, L220303 (2024) / arXiv:2309.14447, "Exact Solution to the Quantum and Classical Dimer Models on the Spectre Aperiodic Monotiling"** — partition function Z = 2^{N_Mystic + 1}, clean Spectre.
- **Shah, Nambiar, Gorshkov, Galitski, arXiv:2503.15588 (2025), "A quantum monomer-dimer model on Penrose tilings"** — a frustration-free Rokhsar–Kivelson model whose ground state is a uniform superposition of all maximal dimer coverings (despite a finite monomer density).
- **Schirmann, Franca, Flicker, Grushin, PRL 132, 086402 (2024) / arXiv:2307.11054** — clean-limit hat zero-mode count, no dilution.
- **Bhola, Damle, arXiv:2311.05634 (2023, rev 2025) and arXiv:2512.23639 (Dec 2025)** — Gallai–Edmonds on periodic lattices (triangular / Shastry–Sutherland / stacked-triangular / octahedral / kagome / star / pyrochlore / claw-free graphs); no aperiodic content.

None combines all three of (i) aperiodic tiling, (ii) site dilution, (iii) maximum-matching/Gallai–Edmonds zero-mode analysis. **The niche is open.**

### Q3 — Matching/dimer structure of aperiodic tilings in math/stat-mech? VERDICT: WELL-STUDIED in the CLEAN limit; UNTOUCHED under dilution.

The clean-limit results are mature and reside in Flicker's group (see Q2). Key benchmarks:
- Rhombic Penrose has no perfect matching; minimum monomer density 81 − 50φ ≈ 0.098 (Flicker–Simon–Parameswaran, PRX 10, 011005 (2020)).
- Dart-kite Penrose has minimum monomer density (7 − 4φ)/5 ≈ 0.106, all of one charge (same paper).
- Ammann–Beenker admits perfect matchings (Lloyd et al. PRB 106, 094202 (2022)).
- Spectre has Z = 2^{N_Mystic + 1} dimer coverings (Singh & Flicker, PRB 109, L220303 (2024)).

These tell us that an *intensive* monomer/zero-mode density (your finding #2: 0 → 0.44 trapped weight fraction) and *extensive* combinatorial structures (#1, #3) are qualitatively expected for an aperiodic substrate. Your novel angle is *not* "the hat has matching structure," it is the *random geometry of Gallai–Edmonds under dilution*, plus the specific scalings of the nullity–deficiency gap, the largest blossom (factor-critical component), and the spanning support.

### Q4 — Do Bhola–Damle anywhere conjecture aperiodic p_c → 0? VERDICT: NO.

Both arXiv:2311.05634 and arXiv:2512.23639 are explicitly periodic-lattice studies. I read the abstract, body, and acknowledgements of 2512.23639 in full: there is **no mention** of aperiodicity, quasiperiodicity, or the Penrose/Ammann–Beenker/Hat/Spectre tilings, and no conjecture about how an aperiodic substrate would shift R-region thresholds. The 2025 kagome paper instead identifies a "claw-free" local-connectivity property that *forces* odd connected clusters to be a single spanning R-region — phenomenology orthogonal to yours. Your aperiodic-substrate p_c → 0 result is therefore not anticipated by any extant Bhola–Damle paper.

### Q5 — Multifractality of HAT eigenstates in 2024–2026? VERDICT: PARTIAL.

- **Penrose and Ammann–Beenker:** multifractal critical states are established. Macé, Jagannathan, Kalugin, Mosseri & Piéchon, PRB 96, 045138 (2017), "Critical eigenstates and their properties in one- and two-dimensional quasicrystals," exhibit exact multifractal ground-state solutions; numerical multifractal analyses by Rieth–Grimm–Schreiber (cond-mat/9809117) and the Jagannathan–Duneau review "Properties of the Ammann–Beenker Tiling and its Square Periodic Approximants," Isr. J. Chem. 64(10–11), e202300119 (4 June 2024; DOI 10.1002/ijch.202300119) summarize the modern state.
- **Hat / Spectre, 2024–2026:** Only one paper performs multifractal/IPR analysis of monotile eigenstates: **Valtýr Kári Daníelsson (University of Iceland) & Helgi Sigurðsson (University of Warsaw), arXiv:2605.29023 (submitted 27 May 2026), "Critical states and anomalous wave transport in an aperiodic polariton monotile."** It uses a 2D *continuum* Schrödinger Hamiltonian with Gaussian polariton scatterers placed on Hat vertices, NOT the vertex-adjacency tight-binding model of Schirmann et al. It extracts D_q and reports super-/sub-diffusion, but does no zero-mode, dilution, or matching analysis.
- A multifractal / IPR scaling of the *vertex tight-binding* eigenstates of the hat (your model), distinguishing compact E=0 modes from putatively critical E≠0 states, is **not yet in the literature.**

### Q6 — Sanity check on the two arXiv IDs. VERDICT: BOTH REAL AND CORRECTLY ATTRIBUTED.

- **arXiv:2512.23639** — Ritesh Bhola & Kedar Damle (TIFR Mumbai), "Random geometry of maximum-density dimer packings of the site-diluted kagome lattice," posted December 2025. The 25YY.NNNNN format reflects arXiv's transition to 5-digit IDs starting January 2025; "25" = 2025, "12" = December. The paper proves that every odd-parity connected cluster of the site-diluted kagome lattice has Gallai–Edmonds decomposition consisting of a single spanning R-region with exactly one monomer, with the proof extending to all claw-free graphs (kagome, star, pyrochlore, hyperkagome). Confirmed by direct HTML fetch.
- **arXiv:2604.21165** — Haitao Gao (UNSW Sydney) & Aaryash Bharadwaj (U. Sydney), "Percolation Critical Probability of Aperiodic Smith Hat tile(1, √3)," v1 posted 23 April 2026. Reports site/bond Bernoulli percolation thresholds p_c^s = 0.822725 ± 0.000044, p_c^b = 0.798161 ± 0.000044 (edge percolation) and 0.544247 ± 0.000101 (site percolation on the dual graph), via Monte Carlo on the 8-kite Smith Hat (1, √3) tile. Purely classical Bernoulli percolation — no Hamiltonian, no zero modes, no matching theory.

Both arXiv IDs verify and the attributions match. Neither preempts your work.

## Details

**Why the contrast in finding #4 is real and publishable.** The Bhola–Damle framework reduces zero-mode geometry to a combinatorial question: which vertices are *essential* in the Gallai–Edmonds sense (forced to be unmatched in some maximum matching). On site-diluted square / honeycomb (PRX 2022) and on site-diluted triangular / Shastry–Sutherland / stacked-triangular / octahedral (arXiv:2311.05634) and on site-diluted kagome (arXiv:2512.23639), R-region percolation is a sharp transition at *finite* vacancy density — sometimes with chaotic self-averaging violations, but always at p > 0. Your numerical observation that on the hat the support of the zero-mode is spanning *only* at p = 0 and shrinks for any finite p (with extrapolated p_c(L) → 0 as L → ∞) is the statement that the hat's vertex graph sits at a kind of "Gallai–Edmonds critical point" in the clean limit, where any dilution destroys the spanning R-region. This has no counterpart in the periodic catalog and is not implied by Gade–Wegner.

**Why findings #1 and #3 are genuinely complementary.** An extensive nullity–deficiency gap (#1) means the adjacency-graph nullity exceeds the matching deficiency by an extensive amount — i.e., the hat's graph carries extra zero modes beyond what bipartite imbalance / minimum-matching arithmetic would predict. This is consistent with the macroscopic clean-limit degeneracy reported by Schirmann et al. but quantifies how that degeneracy redistributes under dilution. The blossom (factor-critical component) #3 is the non-bipartite analogue of the R-region story; its peak at small p ≈ 0.03 has no direct precedent — Bhola–Damle 2311.05634 reports R-region percolation but in a regime *away* from the clean limit, while 2512.23639 gives the different (claw-free) phenomenology where the blossom trivially equals the entire odd cluster. Your peak at p ≈ 0.03 is a new aperiodic-specific feature.

## Caveats

- arXiv IDs 2512.23639 and 2604.21165 use the post-2024 five-digit format. They are correct as given. The Gao–Bharadwaj paper is *only* classical percolation and should be cited as background on hat geometry, not as a quantum / zero-mode reference.
- The König–Ostrovsky-style non-perturbative localization result is technical; the cleanest reference for a non-specialist audience is Brouwer et al. PRB 66, 014204 (2002), plus one sentence explaining the matching-vs-Anderson distinction.
- The classification of the hat's vertex tight-binding model as belonging to a chiral symmetry class is non-trivial: the graph is non-bipartite, so naïve sublattice chiral symmetry is broken, yet zero modes survive — Schirmann et al. PRL 2024 call them "fragile" and note Lieb's theorem fails. This subtlety is itself part of what makes the matching framework the right tool, and is worth one paragraph in your introduction.
- The Daníelsson & Sigurðsson polariton paper (arXiv:2605.29023, submitted 27 May 2026) is recent enough that journal publication may not yet have appeared; confirm publication status before final submission. Note also that the authors are Valtýr Kári Daníelsson (Science Institute, University of Iceland) and Helgi Sigurðsson (Institute of Experimental Physics, University of Warsaw) — two Iceland/Poland-based researchers, not the Grushin or Damle groups.
- "p_c → 0 as L → ∞" should be stated only with the strength your numerics actually support. Reviewers will press on finite-size systematics; the recommended power-law scaling fit above is the natural defense.

## Updated Verdict

**(a) Finding #4 (p_c → 0 for the hat) is GENUINELY NOVEL, not folklore.** It belongs to the matching / Dulmage–Mendelsohn / Gallai–Edmonds framework — the combinatorial support of topologically-protected zero modes — where every published periodic result (square, honeycomb, triangular, Shastry–Sutherland, stacked-triangular, octahedral, kagome) has *finite* p_c. The Gade–Wegner objection ("chiral 2D zero modes are always localized") does not apply, because that statement concerns Anderson localization length of generic random-hopping states under full-lattice random hopping, not the geometric support of the protected zero-mode subspace under site dilution. The collapse of p_c to zero on the aperiodic-monotile substrate is therefore a new qualitative phenomenon within the matching framework, and the strongest publishable claim of your study.

**(b) The "aperiodic monotile + matching-theory" combination is an OPEN NICHE.** No paper combines aperiodic tilings (hat, spectre, Penrose, Ammann–Beenker) with Dulmage–Mendelsohn / Gallai–Edmonds analysis under dilution. The two **direct competitors to acknowledge** are (i) **Roche Carrasco, Schirmann, Mordret & Grushin, PRL 135, 236603 (published 4 December 2025) / arXiv:2505.13304** — Anderson disorder on a Chern-Hat-family model, *not* vacancy or matching; (ii) **Bhola & Damle, arXiv:2512.23639 (Dec 2025)** — Gallai–Edmonds on kagome (claw-free graphs), *not* aperiodic. Neither blocks publication; both should be cited up front to demonstrate awareness. The Damle group itself shows no sign — as of Dec 2025 — of moving toward aperiodic substrates, so the window is currently uncontested, but the cross-group overlap (Flicker is co-author on the original Schirmann hat-PRL *and* the original Flicker–Simon–Parameswaran Penrose-dimer PRX) means this window will not stay open indefinitely.
